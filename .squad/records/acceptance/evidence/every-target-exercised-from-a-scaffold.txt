What theo-cloud does NOT have, stated rather than implied
--------------------------------------------------------
A platform to upload to. The target builds and the artifact runs — measured above, and
its output is byte-for-byte the count the `node` target leaves (29 files each). There is
no TheoCloud service, and no command in this repository sends anything anywhere. That is
a product that does not exist yet, not a defect in the target, and the distinction
matters: a missing platform is closed by building one, while a broken target is closed by
a fix.

Four defects the deno-deploy path cost, each measured with a control
-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. A dot directory is never uploaded. `./.theokit/deno/probe.ts` failed the revision
   where the byte-identical `./visible/probe.ts` served HTTP 200.
2. No import map was emitted, and a prefix cannot stand in for one: Deno refuses
   `"theokit/": "npm:theokit/"` because the portion after the prefix is not URL-joinable
   onto an `npm:` URL. Exact per-specifier entries resolve.
3. `sloppy-imports` was absent; the default template carries six relative imports
   written with a `.js` extension against files on disk ending `.ts`.
4. The emitted instruction named `deployctl`, which talks to the previous Deno Deploy
   platform and answers 401 against a current token.

None was reachable by the 8747-test suite, and the build reported success in every case.
Two hypotheses were refuted along the way and are recorded because a refuted hypothesis
is what makes the surviving one worth something: the dot-directory theory was dismissed
once on a test whose upload still carried node_modules (so it failed for the other
reason), and the pnpm symlink theory was dismissed once against a cached upload that had
not changed at all.
